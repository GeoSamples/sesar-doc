# Object Type

Object type is a controlled list. Use it for the kind of physical object the sample is — for example a core, thin section, or rock hand sample. Terms are grouped in a hierarchy; the web form shows the **full path**. Prefer the most specific term that fits.

Object type is required when you register a sample.

Sample registration API writes expect the **leaf label** — the last part of the path. Batch upload accepts the full path or the leaf label.

If a term you need is missing, [request a term](request-a-term.md).

| Object type | Description |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Analytical preparations | Material sample is a product of processing required for some observation procedure, e.g. thin section, XRF bead, SEM stub, rock powder. If identified separately, this should have a ‘parent’ link to the original sample |
| Analytical preparations > Atom probe tip | Needle-shaped sample milled out of a larger sample with a focused ion beam (FIB). |
| Analytical preparations > Bead | A glass bead formed by fusing a powdered sample with a flux (e.g. lithium tetraborate) at high temperature. Used for major element analysis by X-ray fluorescence (XRF). Sometimes called a fused bead or fusion disc. |
| Analytical preparations > Cell culture | A collection of cells are grown under controlled conditions, generally outside of their natural environment |
| Analytical preparations > Chemical fraction | A fluid concentrating some constituent of interest from a parent sample. The dissolved constituent is actually the sample material of interest. |
| Analytical preparations > Chemical fraction > Eluate | The fluid product that contains the analyte of interest washed from a chromatography column |
| Analytical preparations > Experiment product | Material sample that is the product of an experimental procedure (e.g. synthetic material) |
| Analytical preparations > FIB lamella | Very thin sheet of solid material milled from a larger sample using a focused ion beam. Used for TEM analysis. |
| Analytical preparations > Glass slide smear | Sample from a cell culture (or other microparticulate suspension) spread into a thin layer on a glass slide for optical investigation |
| Analytical preparations > Mechanical fraction | Defined by sample preparation involving mechanical processing, e.g. grain size, density, or grain shape separation. |
| Analytical preparations > Microtome slice | A very thin slice cut from a mounted specimen using a mocrotome or ultramicrotome. |
| Analytical preparations > Mineral separate | An aggregation of particles of the same mineral extracted and concentrated from a rock. Includes hand-picked separates and magentic fractions. |
| Analytical preparations > Mounted specimen | One or more solid objects embedded in a stabilizing matrix, typically epoxy, metal, or paraffin to allow slicing through the mounted object(s). |
| Analytical preparations > Mounted specimen > Polished mounted specimen | Mounted specimen with polished surface exposing mounted material for analysis |
| Analytical preparations > Peel | Acetate peels are made by polishing a planar surface on a sample, etching it with acid to give it some relief, and then chemically melting a piece of acetate onto that surface. The acetate is then pulled off for examination under a microscope. The acetate preserves a fingerprint of the internal structure of the sample surface. Used in paleontology to study complex fossils, e.g. bryozoan. |
| Analytical preparations > Powder | Distinguish from particulate in that particulate is sampled as a micron-size aggregate, whereas this material is ground to a powder for subsequent analysis; it is a powder as a function of some preparation process (e.g. chemical precipitation) |
| Analytical preparations > Pressed pellet | A sample prepared by grinding a parent sample to a fine powder, mixing it with a binder, and pressing the mixture into a die at a pressure of between 15 and 35 tons to produce a solid disc for subsequent analysis, typically by X-Ray fluorescence. |
| Analytical preparations > Residual material | Sample is material remaining after processing to extract some other components of interest from the sample. |
| Analytical preparations > Sectioned specimen | A thin slice of a solid material that has been mounted on a glass slide for study |
| Analytical preparations > Sectioned specimen > Thick section | Thick sections are like thin sections, but milled to a greater thickness. Typcially polished on one or both sides and used for fluid or melt inclusion studies, Raman analyses, and infrared spectroscopy analyses, and SEM or electron microprobe. The standard thickness for a fluid inclusion thick section is 50 micrometers, but thick sections can be made at any thickness. Thick sections can be attached to a glass slide, or can be prepared so that they can be removed from their mount as a stand-alone slice of rock. |
| Analytical preparations > Sectioned specimen > Thin section | Thin sliver of rock cut from a sample with a diamond saw and ground optically flat, and then mounted on a glass slide and ground smooth using progressively finer abrasive grit until the sample is 30 microns thick. |
| Analytical preparations > Sectioned specimen > Thin section > Polished thin section | A thin section that has its free surface polished until perfectly planar and free of pits and scratches. Used for reflected light petrography and for electron microprobe or SEM investigation. |
| Analytical preparations > Sectioned specimen > Ultra thin section | An ordinary thin section that is attached to the glass slide using a soluble cement such as Canada balsam (soluble in ethanol) to allow both sides to be worked on. The section is polished on both sides using a fine diamond paste until it has a thickness in the range of 2-12 microns. This technique has been used to study the microstructure of very fine-grained carbonate rocks, and also in the preparation of mineral and rock specimens for transmission electron microscopy. |
| Analytical preparations > Slab | A relatively planar rock sample, cut from a larger sample to produce a tabular piece of rock with the irregular outline of the original sample on the diameter where the cut was made. |
| Analytical preparations > Squeeze cake | Small piece of whole round core after pore water has been extracted. A 5cm – 15cm piece of core is put in a titanium container and sealed with a fitted piston. The container is then placed on a hydraulic press and pressed under up to 35,000 lbs to extract the pore water. Squeezing destroys the sediment structure |
| Analytical preparations > TEM grid | FIB sections and microtome slices set onto a small grid for handling, transport, and analysis using a transmission electron microscope (TEM). The grid itself can be given a single sample identifier (similar to how there are multiple grains in a grain mount). The linkage from the individual samples in the grid to their parent sample(s) should be documented |
| Analytical preparations > Wedge | A sample prepared by cutting a wedge-shaped piece from a larger solid material, typically to expose a cross-section of internal structure for examination or analysis. |
| Biological samples | Material sample representative of one or more living organisms from a particular biome context, megascopic or microscopic |
| Biological samples > Fossil | Material sample is the remains or trace of one or more organisms preserved in rock; includes whole body, body parts (usually bone or shell), and trace fossils. An organism or organism part becomes a fossil when it has undergone some fossilization process that entails physical and chemical changes akin to diagenesis in a sedimentary rock. Includes trace fossils, which are manifestations of biologic activity preserved in a rock body (typically sedimentary), without included preserved body parts. |
| Biological samples > Human tissue | Sample is tissue or some body part from a human. |
| Biological samples > Organism part | Material sample that is part of an organism, e.g. a tissue sample, plant leaf, flower, bird feather. Include internal parts not composed of organic material (e.g. teeth, bone), and hard body parts that are not shed (hoof, horn, tusk, claw). Hair is tricky, include here for now. Does not necessarily imply existance of parent sample. Not fossilized; generally includes organism parts native to deposits of Holocene to Recent age. |
| Biological samples > Organism product | Material sample is a thing produced by some organism, generally not composed of organic material or including biological tissue, e.g. Shell, antler, egg shell, coral skeleton (organic tissue not included), fecal matter, cocoon, web. Consider internal parts not composed of organic material (e.g. teeth, bone) and hard body parts that are not shed (hoof, horn, tusk) to be organism parts. |
| Biological samples > Whole organism | Material sample consists of the bodies of one or more entire organisms of the same species, from any kingdom. |
| General sample types | A broad category encompassing discrete solid objects, aggregations, fluids, and other material samples that do not fit exclusively into a biological, analytical preparation, or marine and lacustrine context. Includes natural and anthropogenic objects such as rock hand samples, minerals, fossils, artifacts, and mounted specimens, as well as aggregations, fluids in containers, and particulate materials. |
| General sample types > Aggregation | An aggregate material sample that is not biogenic or composed of anthropogenic material fragments. Examples: loose soil or sediment (e.g. in a bag), rock chips, particulate filtrate or precipitate; rock powders. |
| General sample types > Aggregation > Composite sample | A sample composed of multiple pieces, representative of some material, or representative of some site. The pieces do not all originate from the same object. |
| General sample types > Aggregation > Material captured in filter | A material sample captured in filter, for example from a water sample that was filtered. Must be associated with filter size field. |
| General sample types > Artifact | An object made (manufactured, shaped, modified) by a human being, or precursor hominid. Include a set of pieces belonging originally to a single object and treated as a single sample. |
| General sample types > Chip | Individual solid object intentionally broken off a larger solid object sample. A Chip must have a documented parent sample. |
| General sample types > Cube | A sample that is a prepared cube of material, intended as a sample of that material. |
| General sample types > Cylinder | A cylindrical piece of consolidated material not obtained by subsurface drilling. Cores drilled for paleomagnetic analysis are a common example. Tree ring cores are another. |
| General sample types > Dust wipe | A pre-weighed and packaged paper towel (wipe) used to wipe over a surface to collect particulates from the surface |
| General sample types > Filtrate | A sample that has gone through a filtration process to separate solids from fluids (liquids or gases), using a filter medium through which only the fluid can pass. Must be associated with a filter size. |
| General sample types > Gas in container | A material sample that is a gas or gas mixture, necessarily stored inside a container (e.g. gas canister, headspace vial, sealed syringe). May include dissolved gases extracted from fluids or sediments, or gases sampled directly from vents or the atmosphere. |
| General sample types > Individual sample | A single discrete physical object collected and registered as a sample. Represents a bounded, individual unit of material (as opposed to an aggregation, composite, or analytical preparation), Use when the sample is a singular object but no more specific object type applies. |
| General sample types > Liquid in container | A material sample that is a liquid, stored inside a container (e.g. glass vial, bottle, bag). Includes water, brine, melt, or other liquid samples that maintain a liquid phase under ambient storage conditions. |
| General sample types > Meteorite | A meteorite is a solid object that originates in interplanetary space and survives passage through an atmosphere to reach the surface of a planet or moon. |
| General sample types > Mineral specimen | A solid object consisting of one particular mineral, or several minerals intended to be representative of one or more of the mineral species. |
| General sample types > Oriented Core | A core segment for which the azimuthal orientation relative to geographic north has been determined and recorded at the time of drilling, using a downhole orientation tool or scribe marks. Allows structural measurements made on the core to be restored to their in-situ orientation. |
| General sample types > Particle | A small individual solid object that is not one of the other sample types. |
| General sample types > Rock hand sample | Individual piece of rock broken from an outcrop or larger piece of rock. |
| General sample types > Toothpick | A very small sliver or splinter of material removed from a sample using a fine tool. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples | Material samples collected from marine or freshwater lake environments, particularly through seafloor or lakebed drilling, dredging, or water column sampling operations. Includes cores and core-derived products (sections, pieces, half rounds, whole rounds), as well as grabs, dredges, trawls, cuttings, and water samples such as CTD samples. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Boxed core | A collection of core pieces that are stored in an individual box. Typically the box will contain core pieces from the same core. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core | Cylinder of rock or sediment extracted from within the earth, and representing the entire sample extracted during a single drilling event. In many cases the core is extracted in segments that are 'core sections'. A core from a single borehole is rarely a continous unbroken object; commonly parts of the core will break up during drilling or extraction, leaving gaps or sections that are granular material. Cores are normally composed of consolidated ('solid') material, but in some cases loosely consolidated material might be recovered, and considered sediment or tephra. To be called 'core' the material must be sufficiently consolidated to maintain a cylindrical shape. A core hasPart (hasChild) 'Core section'. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core catcher | Material recovered from the bottom of a core barrel by a spring-loaded or basket-style retaining device that prevents core from falling out during retrieval. Core catcher material is often disturbed and may differ in grain size or consolidation from the main core. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core half round | A half-cylindrical piece of consolidated material produced by splitting a core whole round along its axis. One half is typically designated the working half (used for sampling and analysis) and the other the archive half (curated for long-term storage and imaging). |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core piece | A cylindrical piece of consolidated earth material extracted as a single solid object between breaks in recovery of core from a borehole. has parent core section |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core quarter round | A partial cylindrical piece of consolidated material created by along-axis split of a core half round. Has Parent core half round |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core section | Segment of a core representing some interval along the well bore. Child of Core. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core section half | Half-cylindrical piece of consolidated material produced by along-axis split of a core whole round along a selected diameter . Has childOf relation to core section or core, core section, or core piece from which is was split |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core subpiece | A piece of consolidated material broken from a core piece. has Parent core piece or core section |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core U-channel sample | A rectangular prism of loosely consolidated sediment extracted from a core segment. has parent core piece or core segment |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Core whole round | A cylindrical segment of core that has not been split along its axis, retaining the full circular cross-section. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > CTD sample | A water sample collected at a discrete depth using a Niskin or similar bottle mounted on a CTD (conductivity, temperature, depth) rosette. Typically collected to measure physical, chemical, or biological properties of the water column. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Cuttings | Unconsolidated Earth material produced by the grinding action of a drill bit during drilling of a borehole. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Dredge | an aggregation of material sampled by dragging a collection bucket (dredge) across the bottom of a water body |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Grab | A sample of seafloor or lakebed surface sediment or rock collected using a grab sampler (e.g. Van Veen, Smith-McIntyre, or box corer), which closes around the substrate upon contact to retrieve an undisturbed surface sample. |
| Marine and lacustrine samples > Trawl | An aggregation of biogenic or non-biogenic material extracted from a water body |
| Material sample | A material entity that represents an entity of interest in whole or in part (http://rs.tdwg.org/dwc/terms/MaterialSample). Top concept in material sample object type hierarchy. Represents any material sample object. |
| Sampling features | Entities representing locations or contexts where samples were collected such as sites, boreholes, sections, and other spatial or stratigraphic features associated with sampling activities. |
| Sampling features > Hole | A sampling feature that is drilled or dug into the Earth surface to obtain material from the subsurface. Boreholes, soil pits, mine shafts are examples |
| Sampling features > Site | A registered location at which samples were collected or observations occurred. |
| Sampling features > Terrestrial section | Sampling feature is a section of the near-surface Earth, generally in the critical zone. |
